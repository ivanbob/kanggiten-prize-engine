Logotype. Main horizontal
Key elements

Graphic mark: A circular emblem in rich 
purple, located on the left.
Text part: The brand name ˝Kanggiten˛, 
, 
executed in black in a modern font.
Construction and proportions

Composition: A classic horizontal structure (icon + word).
Geometry: The basis of the mark is a perfect circle. Inside it 
are three identically shaped white ˝chevrons˛ that form a rhythmic pattern.
Balance: The height of the graphic mark is visually coordinated with the height of the capital letter ˝K˛, wh
, which 
creates a stable and harmonious horizon line.
01
Meanings of the graphic element

Motion and dynamics: Chevrons directed to the right 
symbolize progress, a vector of development, and speed.
Reference to the name: The shape of the chevrons visually 
resembles the right part of the letter "K," which creates a 
subconscious connection with the name.
Globality: The shape of a circle is associated with integrity, 
community, or global market reach.

Logotype. Main vertical
How to use

The vertical (centric) version of the Kanggiten 
logo is more compact in width, but requires 
more free space above and below. It should be 
chosen in the following cases:
Narrow digital formats

Mobile versions of sites: When there is little horizontal space 
on the smartphone screen, a vertical logo in a ˝cap˛ or on the 
footer looks neater.

Splash screens: When loading mobile applications, a centric composition better keeps the userˇs
s focus.
01
Merch and printed products

Clothing: Print on the chest (T-shirts, hoodies) or on caps. 
Vertical construction fits better into the anatomical 
proportions of the clothing.

Vertical banners (roll-ups): When the layout is stretched 
upwards, this logo helps fill the space logically and 
expressively.

The safe are around the logo is clear space, free from any other graphic elements, text, or 
images, that ensures its visual integrity, legibility, and recognizability.

This term is also known as ˝clear space˛ or ˝exclusion zone˛ and is typically defined by the size of one of the logoˇs own elements (for example, the height of the letter ˝K˛). Adhering to this zone guarantees that the logo will always be noticeable and will not be ˝lost˛ amon
 among

Typography. Basic terms
Weight

Font weight (e.g., bold, semi-bold, thin)
is used to create emphasis and highlight
important elements.
By following these simple 
rules, your texts will always 
look good and professional.
Weight

Font weight (e.g., bold, semi-bold, thin)
is used to create emphasis and highlight
important elements.
Type

Sans-serif fonts create a modern, minimalist,
and clean look.
Harmony

Choose font pairings (styles) that combine
harmoniously, create good visual contrast,
and form a visual integrity.
Hierarchy

Use different font sizes, weights, and styles to 
help create a visual hierarchy. This facilitates 
the perception of information and highlights 
important elements.
Leadin

Using the correct leading ensures
comfortable reading and improves the visual
appearance of the text and the blocks of text
relative to each other.
03
Size

The choice of font size depends on the
purpose of the text and its importance.
Readability

For each layout (size), choose the font 
that ensures comfortable reading and 
improves the perception of information.
Alignment

Text alignment can be left, right, 
centered, or justified. Proper alignment 
improves readability and text structure.
Color

The choice of font color should ensure
contrast with the background and 
improve readability.

hese four patterns demonstrate the evolution of a

A cyclic sequence of three fixed clockwise rotations 
is used to create a complex and non-monotonic 
pattern. The original element with three internal 
lines forming a "K" shape has the following three 
orientations: 00, 900, 1800, 2700.

The vertical (column) and horizontal (row) center-
to-center spacings are the same and constant 
within the grid, as shown in the construction.

Row Offset: The pattern is not a simple, vertically 
aligned grid. The even rows (2nd and 4th) are 
offset horizontally relative to the odd rows (1st and 
3rd). The offset is half the distance between the 
columns, which gives the pattern a characteristic 
"diamond" or "checkerboard" look and adds 
dynamics.
